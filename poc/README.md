# poc/ — proof-of-concept scripts

Scripts here are outside the package (`src/agrijax`) and outside ruff/pyright (`extend-exclude` in
`pyproject.toml`). They are run by hand.

| File | Purpose |
|---|---|
| `coarse_compare.py` | Coarse comparison of Agri-JAX components against an RZWQM2 reference run (CA-TPA 2015). See its docstring. |
| `bench_skeleton.py` | Throughput / gradient-cost skeleton with the computational shape of the PoC model (S-W-type PET, CERES-type crop step, 37-node Brooks-Corey Richards, `sub` sub-steps × `newton` Newton iterations, tridiagonal solve, 3287 days). **Not a model**. |

## bench_skeleton.py

```
uv run python poc/bench_skeleton.py --n 1000 10000 [--days 3287] [--sub 24] [--newton 3] [--solver tridiag|dense]
                                    [--x64 1] [--remat 1] [--grad 1] [--check 1]
```

* `--grad 1` also times one full-parameter reverse-mode gradient (n = first `--n`) and prints `finite=`.
* `--check 1` turns the run into a test: it asserts finite forward outputs, a final pressure head inside
  `[H_MIN, H_MAX]` for every sample, and (with `--grad 1`) a finite gradient for every parameter of every sample;
  a failure raises `AssertionError` (non-zero exit), otherwise the last line is `check: OK`.

CPU is fine for small batches (`--n 4`: 3287 days forward 0.7 s, gradient 3.3 s); GPU numbers were measured on one H100.

### NaN gradients on the GPU (found 2026-09-23, fixed the same day)

**Symptom.** `--grad 1` reported `finite=False` on the H100 for every configuration over 3287 days
(n = 1000 and 10 000; 12×2 and 24×3), while a 60-day CPU test at n = 4 was finite. The forward pass always
reported `nan=False`.

**Diagnosis** (all on CPU, x64, `--sub 12 --newton 2`; scratch scripts, not kept):

1. Lengthening the horizon at n = 4 (365, 1000, 3287 days) stayed finite: the trigger is the *parameter sample*,
   not the horizon. At n = 1000 the gradient is already non-finite at 60 days: `jax.vmap(jax.grad)` per sample
   showed 17/1000 samples with NaN in every parameter except `p1` (the crop parameter, which does not touch the
   soil state). The bad samples have small `lam` (≈0.17) and large `hb` (≈26 cm), i.e. the flattest retention
   curves.
2. Bisection over the horizon must **truncate the forcing**, not the loss: restricting the loss to the first k days
   still back-propagates a zero cotangent through days > k, and `0 * NaN = NaN`, so "grad of the first k days"
   is NaN for every k. With truncated forcing the first bad day for the first bad sample is day 36 (0-based).
3. `checkify.checkify(jax.grad(day_step_loss), errors=checkify.float_checks)` on that day, started from the
   forward state of day 35, reports `nan generated by primitive: sub`. The state of day 35 already has
   `h[0] = -2.6e186 cm`: the **forward iterate had run away long before the gradient failed**. Sub-step by
   sub-step through day 36, `h[0]` goes -6.5e195, -4.6e205, ... -2.5e303 (×1e10 per sub-step), overflows to
   -inf at sub-step 10, and the next Newton update computes `inf - inf` (the `sub`).
4. Why the forward output still says `nan=False`: the Brooks-Corey branches are written as
   `jnp.where(h < -hb, x, 1.0)`; a NaN (or +1e32) head fails the comparison and is read as *saturated*, so
   `theta = ts`, `k = ks`, and the daily outputs are finite (`sw` = 0.453 × 150 cm = 67.95 exactly). Across the
   1000 samples after 60 days, the median sample had every node at `h = +2.5e32 cm` — the whole batch was
   nonphysical, only 17 samples had already overflowed.

**Root cause.** Two bugs and one missing safeguard in the skeleton's Richards step (the retention curve, the
solver and the checkpointing were fine: the tridiagonal system is strictly diagonally dominant even at the
`c = 1e-6` floor, the power-law arguments are guarded, the PET `exp` stays in range):

* Gravity had the wrong sign: `q = -K (dh/dz + 1)` with z positive downward and positive q = downward
  (the bottom boundary is `q = +K`, free drainage) pushes water **up** the column at uniform head.
* The top boundary flux was `-(rain - evap)`: rain left the soil and evaporation entered it, and the evaporation
  demand `pe * min(1, theta/fc)` did not vanish as `theta -> tr`, so it kept extracting water the surface node
  did not have. Together the two drive every sample to a supersaturated or air-dry column.
* The unconverged Newton iteration (2–3 iterations, no line search) had no bound. On a Brooks-Corey curve the
  slope `c = dtheta/dh` vanishes at both ends (`1e-6` floor when saturated, `(-h/hb)^(-lam-1)` when dry), so the
  Newton step `dh = res / (c/DT + ...)` is enormous once the column is pushed beyond the curve, and every
  sub-step starts from the previous overshoot: exponential growth to overflow.

**Fix** (same model shape and cost: two sign changes, one `jnp.clip` on the surface flux, one `jnp.clip` per
Newton iteration):

```python
q   = -kh * ((h_new[1:] - h_new[:-1]) / dz[:-1] - 1.0)                     # gravity drains downward
top = f["rain"] - pe * jnp.clip((theta0 - p["tr"]) / (p["fc"] - p["tr"]), 0, 1)  # + into the soil, supply-limited
return jnp.clip(h_new - dh, H_MIN, H_MAX), None                              # H_MIN=-1e5 cm (pF 5), H_MAX=+10 cm
```

The clamp is what guarantees finiteness for *any* parameter draw: inside `[-1e5, 10]` cm the power-law argument
is bounded by `1e5 / hb_min = 1e4`, so every forward value and every derivative (including the `log(x) * x^-lam`
terms of `d/dlam`) stays representable in float64. The sign fixes are what make the column physical, so the
clamp is (almost) never active: after the fix the final heads lie in [-110, -13] cm over 3287 days.

**Verification** (`--sub 12 --newton 2 --solver tridiag --x64 1 --grad 1 --check 1`):

| Where | n | days | forward | gradient | result |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| CPU (WSL, jax 0.11.2) | 1000 | 60 | 2.1 s | 10.0 s | finite (was 17/1000 NaN) |
| CPU | 4 | 3287 | 0.7 s | 3.3 s | finite, h_final ∈ [-68, -28] cm |
| CPU | 1000 | 1000 | 35 s | — | finite forward, h_final ∈ [-95, -13] cm |
| rorqual H100 (jax 0.10.2, job 21709568) | 1000 | 3287 | 1.83 s (547 sims/s) | 15.5 s | finite, `|g|max = 1.1e-5`, h_final ∈ [-110, -14] cm |
| rorqual H100, job 21709568 | 10000 | 3287 | 8.59 s (1164 sims/s) | 57.6 s | finite, `|g|max = 1.2e-6`, h_final ∈ [-117, -14] cm |

**Review of the fix (2026-09-24).** Signs re-derived from the mixed-form Richards equation with z and q positive
downward, `d theta/dt = -dq/dz - S`, `q = -K (dh/dz - 1)`: the interface flux, the residual
`(theta(h) - theta_old)/DT + (q_out - q_in)/dz + S`, the top flux (`+` = into the soil), the free-drainage bottom
`q = K(h_N)` and the tridiagonal entries (off-diagonals `-K_{i+1/2}/dz^2`, diagonal `C/DT +` their negated sum) all
agree; the tridiagonal matrix equals `jax.jacfwd` of the residual with K held fixed to 1e-17. The iteration is
Newton in theta but lags K (the `dK/dh` part of the Jacobian, about 10 % of its largest entry on a test profile, is
left out), i.e. modified Picard as in RZWQM2. The `[H_MIN, H_MAX]` clamp was never active on 256 samples × 365 days
(12×2 and 24×3). The unconverged iteration is not mass-conservative: summed `|storage change - net flux|` per sample
over 365 days is 0.045 cm median, 0.17 cm max at 12×2, and 4e-5 / 4e-4 cm at 24×3. The formulas assume a uniform
grid (`dz[:-1]` doubles as the node spacing and `dz[:-1] * dz[1:]` in the Jacobian); a non-uniform grid needs the
node spacing and the cell thickness kept apart. Known and left as is, because `exp_inference.py` carries the same
physics and the published measurements were taken on it: the daily `aet` output adds the *potential* soil
evaporation `pe`, not the supply-limited flux applied at the surface.

**Lesson for the real model** (doc 05, lint rule AJ003): a finite forward output is not evidence of a finite
forward *state*; guard `jnp.where` branches on the state variable itself, bound every implicit iterate, and
bisect NaN gradients by truncating the forcing, never by masking the loss.
